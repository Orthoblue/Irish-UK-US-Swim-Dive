# National Centre Limerick Supplies 15 of 68 as Walshe, McSharry and Wiffen Head Swim Ireland’s 2026–27 National Programme

*Dublin, Ireland — Sept. 30, 2026*

Swim Ireland has named 68 swimmers to its National Programme for the 2026–27 season. European champion Ellen Walshe, European medallist Mona McSharry, Olympic champion Daniel Wiffen and World Junior Record holder John Shortt lead the list. The programme, announced Sept. 23, is split into three groups. The 17-strong Performance cohort is aimed mainly at the LA 2028 Olympics. The Senior Pathway, also 17 swimmers, works toward the 2027 European U23 Championships. The Junior Pathway's 34 swimmers target the 2027 European Junior Championships and European Youth Olympic Festival.

Swim Ireland said its entry standards were “deliberately demanding” and based on its On Track system, which benchmarks athletes by event, gender and age against the profiles of future top-16 finishers at World Championships and Olympic Games.

“The standards for inclusion in the National Programme are intentionally challenging, so it is encouraging to see 68 athletes earn their place across the three cohorts,” National Performance Director Andy Reid said.

## National Centres

**National Centre Limerick** has 15 swimmers on the programme, all coached by John Szaranek. Five are in the Performance cohort: Evan Bailey, Jack Cassin, Eoin Corby, Ellie McCartney and Shortt. Seven are on the Senior Pathway: Adam Bradley, Niamh Connery, Ashling McCarthy, Christian O’Brien, Denis O’Brien, Cormac Rynn and Eoghan Rynn. Dylan Mc Grath, Anton Nieuwoudt and Lucy O’Brien are on the Junior Pathway.

**National Centre Dublin**, coached by Steve Beckerleg, has five: Tom Fannon and brothers Daniel and Nathan Wiffen in the Performance group, and Ava Jones and Killian O’Loughlin-Brophy on the Senior Pathway. Coolmine’s Alex Martin, who is listed under the Dublin Pathway with Beckerleg as his coach, is also on the Senior Pathway.

Both centres also won medals at the World Para Swimming European Championships in Kocaeli, Turkey, earlier this month. National Centre Limerick’s Róisín Ní Riain defended her S13 100m backstroke title in 1:06.21 and won silver in the S13 100 freestyle (1:00.52) and SM13 200 IM (2:28.15). National Centre Dublin’s Dearbhaile Brady won silver in the SM6 200 IM (3:20.78), S6 50 butterfly (35.87) and S6 50 freestyle (35.48), and bronze in the SB6 100 breaststroke (1:44.49). Ireland’s seven medals all came from the two of them.

## Clubs

Templeogue SC has five swimmers on the programme. Walshe is in the Performance group under Brian Sweeney, and Dan McBride, AJ Rodriguez Mendoza, Aaron Whelan and Milena Zorychta are on the Junior Pathway. Dolphin SC has three junior pathway swimmers (Sorcha Canning, Lev Lobanets and Tadhg O’Brien). Sundays Well SC (Thomas Bugler, Garvan Gillard), Coolmine SC (Amy Casagrande, Ryan Morgan), Trojan SC (Juliet Cotter, Beau Mc Cabe), Tallaght SC (Lucas Nian, Radu Pamfil) and Celtic Waves SC (Gregory Paramud, Ned Rooney) have two each. Aer Lingus SC’s Julia Dziedzic, Blackrock SC’s Phelim Hanley, NAC SC’s Charlotte Cayuela and Limerick SC’s Meadhbh Ni Riain are also on the Junior Pathway, as are Helge Ho (Aer Lingus/Dublin Pathway) and Jessica Sweeney (Drogheda/Dublin Pathway).

Several southern clubs were also represented in Kocaeli. New Ross SC’s Emersyn Scully reached three finals on her senior debut, Kingdom SC’s Luke O’Donoghue lowered his lifetime best twice to place eighth in the S9 100 backstroke (1:08.42), UL Wolves’ Dylan Prenderville was eighth in the S6 50 butterfly (41.49), and Celtic Waves’ Ellie Lynch was seventh in the S9 100 backstroke (1:23.20).

Auburn’s Matthew Hamilton, from Lisburn, is in the Performance cohort.

## Liffey Swim

Templeogue’s Bruno Delmonte and Tallaght Masters’ Sadhbh Byrne won the 106th Dublin City Liffey Swim on Sept. 12, in 29:50 and 33:05, both on their Liffey Swim debuts. More than 320 swimmers completed the race, which opened the first Dublin City Liffey Festival. Ocean Breakers’ Rachel Lee recorded the fastest women’s time (26:54), and 13-year-old Bruce Healy of Aer Lingus shared the fastest men’s time with Paul Higgins (23:21). NAC Masters won the women’s team prize and Dublin SC the men’s.

## Up next

Walshe and McSharry open the World Aquatics Swimming World Cup in Baku, Azerbaijan, Oct. 1–3. McSharry swims the 200, 100 and 50 breaststroke, and Walshe the 200 butterfly, 400 IM and 200 IM. The series continues in Tashkent (Oct. 8–10) and Astana (Oct. 15–17).
