# McSharry Completes 100 Breast Triple Crown and Nine-for-Nine World Cup Medal Run as Walshe Wins 200 Fly in Astana

*Astana, Kazakhstan — Oct. 17, 2026*

Mona McSharry completed a sweep of the 100m breaststroke at the 2026 World Aquatics Swimming World Cup, winning in Astana in 1:04.03, her fastest swim of the series. With bronze in the 200 and 50 breast, she ended the three-stop short-course (25m) circuit with a medal in all nine races she swam: three gold, two silver and four bronze. Ellen Walshe won the 200 butterfly on the final night and took bronze in the 200 backstroke, finishing the series with three medals.

The final stop ran Oct. 15–17 at Barys Arena.

McSharry topped the 100 breast heats for the third week running, in 1:04.39, then led at every turn from the 50 in the final. She split 30.20 at halfway and 46.86 at the 75 and touched in 1:04.03, 0.73 ahead of Russia’s Evgeniia Chikunova (1:04.76), with the Netherlands’ Tes Schouten third in 1:05.13. The time was 0.19 outside her Irish record of 1:03.84.

“There was definitely a bit of pressure (this week),” McSharry told World Aquatics. “You want to be able to get better and swim faster and I was able to do that, so I’m happy.”

In the 200 breast, McSharry qualified second in 2:22.18 and took bronze in 2:20.46, 0.05 behind Schouten (2:20.41). Chikunova won in 2:16.57, completing her own sweep of the event across the three stops. On the final day, McSharry qualified second for the 50 breast in 30.17 and took bronze in 29.89. Russia’s Ralina Giliazova won in 29.54, with Belgium’s Florine Gaspard second in 29.71.

Walshe produced Ireland’s other win on the last night. She was first at the 100 in 1:00.00 and led the rest of the way in the 200 fly, winning in 2:04.05, 2.07 seconds ahead of China’s Xinyang Zhou (2:06.12). Mohan Chang, who beat Walshe to gold in Baku, was third in 2:06.28. Walshe’s winning time was 1.53 faster than her silver-medal swim in Baku.

Shortly after, Walshe was fourth in the 200 IM in 2:07.00, her fastest 200 IM of the series, after qualifying fifth in 2:14.90. China’s Yiting Yu won in 2:02.63, ahead of Belgium’s Roos Vanotterdijk (2:05.07) and Great Britain’s Abbie Wood (2:05.31).

On Friday, Walshe swam her first 200 backstroke of the series. She qualified sixth in 2:09.79, then was sixth at the 100 in the final (1:02.08) before closing in 15.42 and 15.08 over the last two lengths to take bronze in 2:04.33. RTÉ reported the time was 0.04 outside Melanie Nocher’s Irish record of 2:04.29 from 2011. Canada’s Taylor Ruck won in 2:02.29, with Anna Peplowski second in 2:03.81. Walshe also qualified sixth for the 100 IM in 1:00.90 but did not contest the final, RTÉ reported.

Walshe opened the meet with fourth in the 400 IM in 4:29.27. Yu won in 4:22.74, ahead of Japan’s Waka Kobori (4:26.99) and Wood (4:27.38).
