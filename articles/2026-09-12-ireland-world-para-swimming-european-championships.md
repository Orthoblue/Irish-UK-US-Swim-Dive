# Ní Riain Defends European 100 Back Title as Brady Wins Four Medals and Ireland Takes Seven in Kocaeli

*Kocaeli, Turkey — Sept. 12, 2026*

Ireland left the World Para Swimming European Championships, Sept. 7–12 in Kocaeli, with seven medals: one gold, five silver and one bronze. Róisín Ní Riain of National Centre Limerick successfully defended her European S13 100m backstroke title, and Dearbhaile Brady of National Centre Dublin collected four medals from four finals. Ireland placed 16th on the medal table.

The 10-strong team included six swimmers making their senior international championship debuts. National Para Swimming Head Coach Hayley Burke said the team recorded 19 personal bests across the week.

**Irish medals** (official results)

| Medal | Event | Swimmer | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gold | W 100m backstroke S13 | Róisín Ní Riain | 1:06.21 |
| Silver | W 100m freestyle S13 | Róisín Ní Riain | 1:00.52 |
| Silver | W 200m IM SM13 | Róisín Ní Riain | 2:28.15 |
| Silver | W 200m IM SM6 | Dearbhaile Brady | 3:20.78 |
| Silver | W 50m butterfly S6 | Dearbhaile Brady | 35.87 |
| Silver | W 50m freestyle S6 | Dearbhaile Brady | 35.48 |
| Bronze | W 100m breaststroke SB6 | Dearbhaile Brady | 1:44.49 |

Ní Riain qualified first for the 100 back final in 1:10.03, then dropped almost four seconds at night to win in 1:06.21, which Paralympics Ireland reported as a personal best. She was 2.80 seconds clear of Italy’s Carlotta Gilli (1:09.01), with Great Britain’s Ursula Carroll third in 1:10.56.

Gilli edged Ní Riain twice. In the S13 100 free, Ní Riain qualified third in 1:01.85 and took silver in 1:00.52, 0.31 behind Gilli (1:00.21) and ahead of Spain’s Emma Feliu Martin (1:00.92). In the SM13 200 IM, Ní Riain led Gilli at the 150 (1:51.50 to 1:52.45) before Gilli came home to win in 2:26.43; Ní Riain took silver in 2:28.15, more than 10 seconds faster than her 2:38.44 heat, with Russia’s Sofiia Mezhinskaia third in 2:30.76.

Ní Riain also finished fourth twice: in the SB13 100 breaststroke in 1:18.96, 0.80 from bronze, and in the S13 50 free in 28.49, after qualifying fourth in 28.72. She was fifth in the S13 400 free in 4:49.19.

Brady’s week began with silver in the SM6 200 IM in 3:20.78, behind Germany’s Verena Schott (3:07.52). She led the S6 50 butterfly final at halfway and took silver in 35.87, 0.33 behind Italy’s Giulia Terzi (35.54). She was the top qualifier in the S6 50 free in 34.97 and took silver in 35.48, 0.22 behind Switzerland’s Nora Meister (35.26).

Brady closed the championships with bronze in the SB6 100 breaststroke in 1:44.49, more than two and a half seconds under the 1:47.03 she swam in the morning heats, which Paralympics Ireland reported as a lifetime best. Ukraine’s Veronika Korzhova won in 1:41.30 and Hungary’s Evelin Szaraz was second in 1:43.23.

Emersyn Scully of New Ross SC reached three finals on her senior debut. She was sixth in the S14 100 backstroke in 1:16.68, after 1:16.72 in the heats; sixth in the SB14 100 breaststroke in 1:24.90, after qualifying eighth in 1:25.89; and eighth in the SM14 200 IM in 2:44.30, after 2:45.08 in the heats.

Kingdom SC’s Luke O’Donoghue lowered his lifetime best twice in the S9 100 backstroke, qualifying eighth in 1:08.74 and placing eighth in the final in 1:08.42. UL Wolves’ Dylan Prenderville set a lifetime best of 41.31 to qualify for the S6 50 butterfly final, where he was eighth in 41.49. Celtic Waves’ Ellie Lynch, also on her debut, was seventh in the S9 100 backstroke final in 1:23.20.

Limavady’s Elodie Bateson was 11th in the S11 50 free heats in 39.60 and 10th in the S11 100 free heats in 1:30.49. Blackrock’s Luke Brennan was 10th in both the S8 50 free (28.77) and SM8 200 IM (2:46.60) heats.

Lisburn City’s Deaten Registe was fourth in the SB14 100 breaststroke in 1:08.36, 0.29 from bronze, and clubmate Síomha Nic Brádaigh reached three S7 finals, placing fifth in the 200 IM (3:27.78) and sixth in both the 100 back (1:34.00) and 100 free (1:23.20).
