# Zeller, Bartee and Komoroczy Break Auburn Records as Tigers Finish 2nd and 3rd at Wolfpack Elite Invitational

*Greensboro, N.C. — Nov. 21, 2026*

Auburn left the Greensboro Aquatic Center with three school records, a men’s 200 freestyle win and a pair of top-three team finishes at the Wolfpack Elite Invitational, Nov. 18–21.

The 19th-ranked Tiger women closed within 74.5 points of host and eighth-ranked NC State, taking second with 1,701.50 points. The 14th-ranked men were third with 1,247, finishing 38.5 behind Virginia Tech and 14.5 ahead of Arizona. George Washington rounded out the five-team field.

Senior Zoey Zeller became the first Auburn woman under 2:08 in the 200 breaststroke, taking second in a school-record 2:07.99. The final and her 2:08.66 prelim were NCAA and U.S. Open cuts. She also placed third in the 200 IM (1:58.89; her 1:58.53 prelim was an NCAA cut) and second in the 100 breast in 59.87, another NCAA and U.S. Open time.

Freshman Zetta Grace Bartee lowered her own school record in the 200 butterfly by 0.65 seconds, winning in 1:53.79. Official coverage called her 1:55.78 prelim the 20th-fastest time in program history. She was second in the 200 IM in 1:57.72, the 10th-fastest time in Auburn history, and fourth in the 100 fly in 52.19, which official coverage said made her the 10th-fastest Tiger in that event. All three finals were NCAA and U.S. Open cuts.

Junior Lora Komoroczy broke Aly Tetzloff’s seven-year-old 100 backstroke record of 50.92 with a 50.86 leadoff on the 400 medley relay, becoming only the second Tiger under 51 seconds in the event. She was 0.02 off that mark in the individual final (second, 50.88), won the 200 back in a personal-best 1:53.52 — the 10th-fastest time in program history — and was third in the 100 fly in 52.09. Those individual swims were NCAA and U.S. Open cuts.

Sophomore Daniel Krichevsky supplied Auburn’s only individual event win, taking the men’s 200 free in 1:33.35, the 22nd-best time in program history and his fifth mark on the event’s top-25 list. It was a U.S. Open cut and missed the NCAA standard of 1:33.29 by 0.06. Gabriel Machuco was second in 1:34.57 and also second in the 200 IM (1:43.72; his 1:43.55 prelim was an NCAA cut).

Bradford Johnson and Mike Rice went 2-3 in the 100 fly in 45.29 and 45.37, behind Arizona’s Haakon Naughton (45.22). Official coverage called Johnson’s time the ninth-best in school history and said it made him the seventh-fastest Tiger in the event. Rice had led prelims in 45.65, a 10th-all-time performance; his 45.37 final is the 16th-best time in program history and moved him to ninth among Auburn performers. Both finals were NCAA and U.S. Open cuts.

Warner Russ broke 19 seconds for the first time, taking third in the 50 free in a personal-best 18.90, the ninth-fastest swim in Auburn history and an NCAA and U.S. Open cut. Rice (42.15) and Russ (42.26) also cleared the NCAA cut in the 100 free, as did Alexey Glivinsky’s 42.18 prelim. Kaua Carvalho led 100 breast prelims in a personal-best 52.11 — official coverage said that made him the seventh-fastest Tiger in the event — then was third in 52.18, both NCAA cuts. His 1:53.93 200 breast prelim was the 24th-best time and sixth-fastest performer in program history; Maston Ballew took third in that final in 1:53.99. Ivan Tarasov was third in the 100 back in 45.73, an NCAA cut. Sammie Hamilton was second in the 500 free in 4:44.07, an NCAA and U.S. Open cut that SwimSwam reported was 0.43 off her lifetime best.

The men went 1-2 in the 200 medley relay, both under the NCAA qualifying standard of 1:23.52. Luke Bedsole (21.35), Uros Zivanovic (22.89), Johnson (20.11) and Russ (18.30) won in 1:22.65, the fifth-fastest time in program history. The B-relay of Tarasov (21.12), Carvalho (23.18), Rice (19.97) and freshman Matthew Hamilton (18.65) was second in 1:22.92, the ninth-fastest time in school history. The men were second in the 400 free relay in 2:48.23, the sixth-fastest time in program history and under the NCAA cut of 2:48.73 (Russ 42.35, Rice 41.38, Glivinsky 41.99, Krichevsky 42.51). The 800 free relay was third in 6:15.76, an NCAA provisional and 0.17 off Auburn’s 10th-fastest time (Krichevsky 1:33.72, Bedsole 1:33.47, Machuco 1:33.74, Glivinsky 1:34.83). The 200 free relay was second in 1:16.13, an NCAA provisional that missed the automatic cut of 1:16.02 by 0.11 (Russ 19.06, Rice 18.95, Hamilton 19.33, Bedsole 18.79).

The women were second in the 400 medley relay in 3:31.57, an NCAA provisional, on Komoroczy’s record 50.86 plus splits of 1:00.07 from Zeller, 52.65 from Bartee and 47.99 from Rosalie Reef. They were also second in the 800 free relay in 7:08.01 (Bella Ekk 1:46.63, Maggie McGuire 1:47.21, Wyllo Hanson 1:47.91, Bartee 1:46.26).

Hamilton finished seventh in the 100 fly A-final in 46.88 (prelim 46.81), 0.12 outside the U.S. Open cut of 46.69. His 43.19 100 free prelim missed the U.S. Open cut of 43.09 by 0.10; he was 15th in 43.45. He made the U.S. Open cut in 50 free prelims (19.67) and placed 15th in 19.87.

Auburn’s divers competed the same week at the Tennessee Diving Invitational in Knoxville. Select swimmers are next at the Toyota U.S. Open, Dec. 2–5, in Austin.
