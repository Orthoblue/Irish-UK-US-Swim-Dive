# McSharry Wins 100 Breast Again and Takes Another Full Set of Medals at Tashkent World Cup; Walshe Reaches Five Finals

*Tashkent, Uzbekistan — Oct. 10, 2026*

Mona McSharry won the 100m breaststroke for the second week running at the World Aquatics Swimming World Cup in Tashkent, Oct. 8–10, and again medalled in all three breaststroke events. She added silver in the 50 and bronze in the 200 at the second stop of the short-course (25m) series. Ellen Walshe reached five finals without a medal, placing fourth in both individual medleys.

McSharry now has six medals from six swims on the 2026 circuit, with two wins in the 100 breast. A third 100 win in Astana next week would complete the Triple Crown in the event.

McSharry was again the top qualifier in the 100 breast, in 1:04.79, and won the final in 1:04.25. She turned first at the 50 in 30.32 and led from there, finishing 0.25 ahead of Russia’s Evgeniia Chikunova (1:04.50). Belgium’s Florine Gaspard and the Netherlands’ Tes Schouten tied for third in 1:05.38. The time was 0.04 slower than her winning swim in Baku.

In the 50 breast, McSharry qualified second in 30.03 and took silver in 29.85, 0.09 behind Russia’s Ralina Giliazova (29.76) and 0.01 ahead of Gaspard (29.86). The same three swimmers made the podium in Baku a week ago, in a different order.

McSharry opened the meet on Thursday with bronze in the 200 breast in 2:20.41. Chikunova won in 2:17.23, and Schouten took silver in 2:20.07.

Walshe’s best results came in the medleys. She qualified sixth for the 200 IM final in 2:19.23, then went 2:07.74 for fourth, 0.88 off the podium. China’s Yiting Yu won in 2:03.34, ahead of Great Britain’s Abbie Wood (2:06.53), with Rebecca Meder third in 2:06.86. In the 400 IM, Walshe was fourth in 4:32.71; Yu won in 4:27.09, with Wood second in 4:28.90 and Japan’s Waka Kobori third in 4:29.02.

Walshe was fifth in the 200 butterfly in 2:08.69, the event in which she took silver in Baku. Yu won in 2:02.75, ahead of Finland’s Laura Lahtinen (2:05.20) and China’s Mohan Chang (2:05.61).

Walshe also raced both sprint butterfly events. She qualified eighth for the 50 fly final in 25.94 and was eighth in 26.00, then qualified seventh for the 100 fly in 58.19 and placed eighth in the final in 58.22. Belgium’s Roos Vanotterdijk won both, in 24.75 and 55.20.

The series concludes at the third and final stop in Astana, Kazakhstan, Oct. 15–17, where McSharry and Walshe are both due to race.
