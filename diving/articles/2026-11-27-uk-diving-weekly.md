# Noah Williams Wins Senior Platform as Dive London Leads Home Divers at London Legacy Open

*London, England — Nov. 27, 2026*

The London Legacy Open in Stratford, east London, on Nov. 12 was the biggest British diving meet of the month. It drew 215 divers, including teams from Germany, Croatia, Sweden, Ukraine and New Zealand. Dive London Aquatics Club won all three senior men's individual titles. German clubs won three of the four synchro events and half of the junior platform titles.

Noah Williams won senior men's platform with 488.50, the second-highest total of the meet, ahead of clubmate Robbie Lee (465.60). Matthew Dixon won both senior men's springboards, 1m in 357.25 and 3m in 398.05, with Hugo Thomas second on 3m (392.75). Evie Smith won senior women's 3m for Dive London in 278.45. Ukraine's Anna Arnautova took senior women's 1m (239.45), and Berliner TSC's Rhoda Schneegass won platform (241.10).

## Junior groups

Caitlyn Coster of Dive London won both A-group (16–18) girls' springboards. She took 1m in 366.80 and 3m by 0.25 over Germany's Henni Mehner (410.30 to 410.05). City of Leeds' Livia Thomas won girls' platform in 364.75, and Edinburgh's Scarlet D'Mellow was second (348.90). In the boys' A group, Luke Raggett won 1m for Dive London in 442.55, with Sheffield's Robbie Wood second (420.85), and lost 3m by three points to Germany's Arthur Fleck (488.15 to 485.15). SC DHfK Leipzig's Nuncio Steffenhagen posted the top score of the meet, 511.60, to win A-group platform.

In the B group (14–15), Southampton Diving Academy's Imogen Poole won girls' 1m (290.00), Dive London's Sienna Robson won 3m (324.20) and Sheffield's Willow Kesteven-McGrath won platform (287.95). In the C group, Plymouth's Seth Greentree won boys' platform in 273.90.

On synchro, Southend's Connor Lano and Todd Geggus won men's 3m in 370.59. Jorden Fisher-Eames (Dive London) and Robbie Wood (Sheffield) were second on men's platform synchro (320.91), and Edinburgh's Jessica Nearn and Scarlet D'Mellow were second on women's platform synchro (236.19). Leipzig pairs won women's 3m and both platform synchro events.

## Masters and novices

The GBDF Winter Masters Championships were held in Southend-on-Sea on Nov. 14, with 88 divers. Southend's Thomas Hadjicostas posted the top score, 229.60, to win men's 16–29 1m. Maidstone's Sam Harvey won 16–29 3m (224.35). Royal Air Force SA's Charlotte Goodman won women's 16–29 3m (214.80) and platform (205.40). Manchester's Philip Bowers won the men's 30–39 3m and platform, and Sheffield's Shaun Tesh won all three men's 50–59 boards.

The Peter Waterfield Novices in Southampton on Nov. 21 drew 105 young divers. Southend Diving's Isobel Stefanovic had the top score of the day, 291.55, in girls' Group B+. Eleanor Rees of Star Diving Club Guildford was second (289.05). Southend also won girls' Groups C (Halle Watson, 254.30) and E (Ella-Grace McCardle, 278.20). Plymouth won boys' Groups D (Finley Andrew-Mundy, 268.35) and E (Sammy Cowell, 239.85).

## British divers abroad

Aidan Heslop was the top British finisher at the World Aquatics High Diving World Cup in Zhaoqing, China (Nov. 21–22). He placed fourth in the men's 27m with 358.90 after ranking third in both prelims and semifinals. Romania's Constantin Popovici won by 0.55 over James Lichtenstein of the United States.

## Awards

At the Aquatics GB Awards in Glasgow on Nov. 7, Maisie Bond was named Diving Athlete of the Year after winning individual and synchro 10m platform gold at the European Championships in Paris. Tom Owens won Diving Coach of the Year for the second year running, and Jorden Fisher-Eames was Diving Emerging Athlete of the Year.
