# Hedberg Hits 470 on Platform as Five College Divers Top 450 at Midseason Invitationals

*United States — Nov. 27, 2026*

The week of Nov. 17–22 was the busiest of the fall for college diving. Midseason invitationals ran in Knoxville, Columbus, Athens, Iowa City, Columbia (Mo.), Houston and Princeton, and five men's finals scores went over 450 points.

Indiana freshman Joshua Hedberg won men's platform at the Tennessee Invitational with 470.15. No one has scored higher in any of the 171 college meets with official results on this site's diving dashboard this season. Hedberg qualified first at 431.30, and North Carolina's Ciro Mejia was second in the final with 418.60. Tennessee's Thomas Ciprik won 3-meter in 457.20, and teammate Bennett Greene won 1-meter in 424.20. Ciprik and Greene finished 1-2 on both springboards.

## Purdue sweeps the boards in Columbus

At the Ohio State Fall Invitational, Purdue senior Holden Higbie won men's 3-meter in 453.25, nearly 60 points clear of teammate Tyler Wills (393.70). Higbie also shared the 1-meter title with sophomore Nathaniel Grannis. Both scored 396.90, and the official results mark the tie with an asterisk. Wills won platform in 454.60, half a point ahead of Michigan's Jaxon Bowshire (454.10), with Louisville's Ray Pres third (387.65).

Purdue junior Avery Worobel won both women's springboards, 1-meter in 327.85 and 3-meter in 348.30, so the Boilermakers won all four springboard titles in Columbus as well as men's platform. Ohio State's Mia Prusiecki won women's platform in 329.90 for the hosts.

## Florida and Georgia split the honors in Athens

At the UGA Fall Invitational (Nov. 19–22), Georgia's Matthew Bray won men's 3-meter in 442.50, ahead of Florida's Miguel Tovar (415.05). Renato Calderaro took 1-meter for the hosts in 369.20, and Florida's Jesus Gonzalez won platform in 390.50. Florida's Camyla Monroy swept the women's springboards (1-meter 331.00, 3-meter 371.20), and Duke's Joise Zillig won platform in 283.10.

## Elsewhere

- **Tennessee Invitational (Knoxville, Nov. 17–20):** Tennessee's Desharne Bent-Ashmeil won both women's springboards (1-meter 337.40, 3-meter 370.65). North Carolina's Sofia Knight won platform in 314.20.
- **Hawkeye Invitational (Iowa City, Nov. 17–20):** Rutgers' Bailee Sturgill won women's 1-meter (305.35) and 3-meter (349.35) and was second on platform behind Iowa's Katie Parker (294.75).
- **Mizzou Invitational (Columbia, Mo., Nov. 17–20):** Missouri's Tanner Braunton won men's 1-meter (352.85) and 3-meter (397.75). South Carolina's Noah Stasik took platform in 400.50. Kansas went 1-2 on women's 1-meter through Shiyun Lai (322.55) and Sijia Li (321.45).
- **Phill Hansel Invitational (Houston, Nov. 17–21):** Houston's Michelle McLeod won women's 1-meter (304.35) and platform (293.50). Air Force's Helena Gibson won 3-meter in 321.55.
- **Princeton Big Al Invitational (Nov. 20–22):** Princeton's Aidan Wang won both men's springboards (1-meter 355.00, 3-meter 388.40), and Charlotte Norman did the same for the Princeton women (3-meter 342.10, 1-meter 293.10). Columbia's Will Cooley won men's platform in 427.00.
- **Terrier Invitational (Boston, Nov. 20–22):** UMass's Carson Harrington won men's 1-meter (368.50) and 3-meter (431.65).

## Auburn

Auburn sent only its divers to Knoxville, and they came away with three podiums. Peder Hubred was third on men's 3-meter (396.95, after a 405.10 prelim), Juliette Landi was third on women's 3-meter (352.70) and Emily Hallifax was second on platform (285.95). Ethan Swart was fourth on 1-meter (364.80) and fifth on both 3-meter and platform. Aubie's full recap is on the site.

## Americans on the international stage

At the World Aquatics High Diving World Cup in Zhaoqing, China (Nov. 21–22), James Lichtenstein of the United States finished second in the men's 27m with 428.55, 0.55 behind Romania's Constantin Popovici (429.10). Lisa Faulkner was fourth in the women's 20m (302.20), which Australia's Rhiannan Iffland won in 335.60.

## Up next

USA Diving's Winter National Championships run Dec. 11–15 at COM Aquatics in Midland, Texas, after the national qualifier on Dec. 9–10. Auburn's divers are scheduled to compete.
